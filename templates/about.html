01

Project Objective

Type 2 diabetes mellitus is a global chronic health crisis affecting over 500 million people worldwide. Crucially, up to 90% of individuals with pre-diabetes remain undiagnosed because symptoms develop insidiously over many years.

The objective of DiabetesCare AI is to construct an accessible, non-invasive early-warning screening tool using machine learning. By evaluating 21 readily accessible behavioral, biometric, and demographic indicators, the system identifies individuals at high risk and encourages timely clinical diagnostic laboratory tests (such as HbA1c or Fasting Blood Glucose).

The CDC BRFSS Dataset

The dataset utilized is the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS 2015), curated annually by the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). BRFSS is the world's largest continuously conducted telephone health survey, gathering health behaviors, chronic conditions, and healthcare access indicators from residents across all 50 states.

03

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) Highlights

Our exploratory analysis revealed pivotal clinical patterns across the surveyed population:

  • Class Imbalance: 86.07% non-diabetic (Class 0) vs. 13.93% diabetic/pre-diabetic (Class 1), representing a 6.18:1 ratio in the raw data.
  • The Power of Self-Rated Health: General health rating (GenHlth) emerged as the single highest correlated feature ($r = +0.294$). The diabetes prevalence in respondents rating their health "Poor" is 37.89%, compared to just 2.52% in those rating "Excellent".
  • Age-Related Risk Acceleration: Diabetes risk increases monotonically from 1.37% (ages 18–24) to 21.85% (ages 70–74), confirming age as a fundamental physiological vulnerability.
  • Cardiometabolic Comorbidities: Individuals with high blood pressure exhibited a 24.45% diabetes rate (vs 6.04% for normotensive respondents), illustrating the tight link between arterial health and glycemic metabolism.
04

Data Preprocessing & Data Leakage Prevention

To ensure strict scientific validity and prevent overly optimistic evaluation scores:

1. Deduplication Analysis

Identified 24,206 duplicate rows. Analysis showed that 98.65% of duplicates were healthy (Class 0) respondents with identical discrete survey choices. Removing them prevented test-set memorization (data leakage) and improved positive class density from 13.93% to 15.29% without losing diverse health patterns (final clean rows: 229,474).

2. Stratified 80:20 Split

Used stratify=y with random_state=42 to strictly guarantee that both the training set (183,579 rows) and test set (45,895 rows) maintain identical 15.29% positive proportions.

3. Standard Scaling (Without Leakage)

A StandardScaler was fitted strictly on X_train and saved to scaler.joblib. Linear models consumed scaled data; tree-based models consumed clean unscaled data.

05

Machine Learning Algorithms Compared

Four diverse model families were trained using class-balanced weighting (class_weight='balanced') and evaluated on 45,895 unseen test patients:

Logistic Regression

L2-regularized linear baseline. Fast (1.19s), achieves 76.01% recall and 0.8106 ROC-AUC, but limited by linear decision boundaries.

Decision Tree Classifier

Single pruned CART tree (max depth 10). Interpretable flowchart rules; achieved 77.19% recall and 0.8015 ROC-AUC.

Random Forest Classifier

Ensemble of 100 balanced decision trees with bagging. Solid 77.82% recall, 0.8173 ROC-AUC, and 0.4426 PR-AUC.

HistGradientBoosting Classifier 🏆

Scikit-learn's modern histogram-based gradient boosting algorithm. Won across all critical discrimination and screening metrics.

06

Why HistGradientBoosting Was Selected

In healthcare triage, Recall (Sensitivity) and PR-AUC are paramount. A model that misses diabetic individuals poses severe risks to patients who miss early intervention opportunities.

Highest Recall Identified 79.13% of all diabetic cases at default threshold (up to 85.11% with tuning), outperforming all other models.
Highest ROC-AUC (0.8186) Best ability to rank a randomly chosen diabetic individual higher than a non-diabetic individual.
Highest PR-AUC (0.4454) Significantly outperformed Logistic Regression (0.4121) and Decision Tree (0.4163) on the imbalanced positive class.

07

Inner Validation Threshold Calibration

Standard machine learning models classify any probability $\ge 0.50$ as positive. However, in medical screening where false negatives are critical, default 0.50 cutoffs miss too many early cases.

We carved out an inner validation set (36,716 samples) exclusively from training data and evaluated 81 potential cutoffs to maximize the $F_2$-score (giving twice the weight to Recall over Precision).

Outcome: Calibrating the threshold to 0.43 increased screening recall on the unseen test set from 79.13% to 85.11%, cutting missed diabetic diagnoses by 28.7% (from 1,465 down to 1,045).
08

Model Limitations & Ethical Considerations

Responsible AI deployment requires transparent disclosure of model boundaries:

  • Survey-Based Self-Reporting: BRFSS relies on telephone interview responses. Self-reported weight, height, and general health are susceptible to recall bias and social desirability bias.
  • Cross-Sectional Nature: The dataset is observational and cross-sectional. Correlations identified by the model do not establish direct medical causality.
  • Screening vs. Diagnosis: A statistical risk prediction of 75% does not mean an individual is definitively diabetic—it means their profile strongly matches demographic cohorts with high diabetes incidence.
Clinical Disclaimer: DiabetesCare AI is an educational technology demonstration. It does not provide medical diagnoses, treatment prescriptions, or formal medical clearance. Users must consult licensed physicians for clinical blood tests (such as HbA1c or oral glucose tolerance tests).
